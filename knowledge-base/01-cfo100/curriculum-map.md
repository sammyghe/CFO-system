## The programme

A joint effort of **Renew Capital** with **Global Affairs Canada** and the **Norwegian
Agency for Development Cooperation (Norad)**, to increase the *credibility, quantity and
quality of finance professionals in Africa*.

**Stated objectives:**
- Graduate a class of finance professionals that have become or can become CFOs
- Build a cohort of peers that stay connected and build each other into stronger finance
  professionals
- Ensure the class understands **the tools and habits of CFOs**

**Logistics:** 24 Aug – 26 Oct 2026 · Mondays 16:00–17:30 EAT (15:00–16:30 CAT) · Zoom ·
assignments submitted on the training platform · recordings saved there for catch-up.
Platform: `https://training.renewcapital.com/course/cfo100-2026`
Zoom: `https://us02web.zoom.us/webinar/register/WN_55-Cog75TtKfz5opRYsJFg`

## Grading — read this carefully

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| **Final exam** | **50%** |
| **Attendance** | **40%** |
| Assignment completion | **10%** |

- Certificate at **65% or higher**.
- Attendance credit for a session requires attending **at least half** of it.
- **Session 9 (Sustainable Finance / Review) counts for bonus credit and makes up for a
  missed class.**

**Attendance floor — Renew states it two ways, and the decks are the later word:**
- Welcome email (21 Aug) and syllabus: *"attend at least 8 of 10"*.
- **Session 3 and Session 4 decks (7 and 14 Sep): "You must attend at least 7 of the 9 live
  sessions (the 10th session is the Final Exam)."**

The decks are more recent and more specific. Treat **7 of 9** as the operative floor, with
Session 9 able to cover a miss. Confirm with Emily if it ever gets close.

**What this means in practice:** the exam and showing up are 90% of the grade. A weak
homework is worth a fraction of one-tenth. That is not an argument for skipping the work —
the homework is how the habits get built, and the exam tests the same material — but it
does mean a thin Session 4 submission is not the thing to worry about. The exam is.

## Session detail

### Session 1a — Introduction
Purpose, objectives and format of CFO100; expectations for participation; what becoming an
executive and a CFO involves.

### Session 1b — The CFO: Roles, Responsibilities and Skills
Objectives: explain what a CFO does · understand reporting, liquidity, and financial
planning and analysis · understand the **soft skills** a CFO needs.

**Homework — Exercise 1:** read the job descriptions of a **CFO, Controller and Finance
Manager** · read the functions of an accounting department · **document your time**.
**Exercise 2:** read the **CFO Checklist** · **implement the Exec disciplines**.

> The job-description material is captured in `02-mentality/cfo-role.md` and mapped against
> what Maji Safi actually has today.

### Session 2 — Overseeing the Controller and Accounting Functions
Objectives: what an accounting system should look like · the key components of a good one ·
how to manage it with checklists.

**Homework:** read the finance department checklists · prepare **daily, weekly and monthly
checklists for the accounting process** · **begin implementing the daily and weekly ones**.

> The source material is Renew's *Accounting and Finance Team Checklist* (Finance Manager /
> Senior Accountant / Cashier–Junior Accountant, across daily → annual). Note the homework
> verb: *begin implementing*. Not read. See `02-mentality/operating-rhythm.md` (not yet
> built) and drawback D-05.

### Session 3 — Financial Statement Analysis
Objectives: why, what and how to analyse financial statements · the ratios and margins every
executive should calculate and understand · **to start doing it**.

**Homework:** gather your most recent financial statements · prepare **common size
analysis**, **strength ratios**, **performance ratios** · be prepared to share observations.

**Status: done.** Answers in the Drive doc "Ema sign". This session left a permanent artefact
in the Year-1 model — the Key Ratios tab is built as "the four families from CFO 100
Session 3". The reflection honestly recorded having no external benchmark set (D-18), and
concluded "on paper: strong" (D-20).

### Session 4 — Financial Reporting and Variance Analysis · 14 Sep
Objectives: understand **why reporting is important** · explain what a **great financial
report** looks like · learn how to **prepare** one.

**Homework:**
1. Get your most recent financial statements
2. Prepare: **financial ratios · graphs · financial commentary with recommendation**
3. **Prepare a summary report for your CEO**

**Plus a Case Study assignment that week** — *"You will be evaluated on your ability to
identify the problem, craft a solution and present it."*

**This is the session that was done thinly, and now it is clear why.** Session 4 is not more
ratio arithmetic — Session 3 already did that. Session 4 is the **communication** half of
the job: graphs, written commentary, a recommendation, and a report addressed to a reader.
Carrying Session 3's ratio answers forward would miss the entire point of it. See D-19 and
the new D-21 (variance analysis needs actuals, and there are none).

### Session 5a — Managing Cash · today, 21 Sep
Objectives: why cash flow management is important · what good cash management looks like ·
how to manage cash · how to forecast cash (tee-up for 5b) · **the common mistakes and
misconceptions about cash management**.

### Session 5b — Cash Forecasting · today, 21 Sep
Objectives: why cash forecasting matters · what good cash forecasting looks like · how to
forecast cash (**13-week cash flow**, pro forma statement forecast) · common misconceptions.

**Homework (three parts):**
1. Use the **"Managing Cash Well Checklist"** at your company. Review whether an **internal
   controls checklist for cash** is in place. Identify any misconception or mistake in cash
   management. **Propose alternative solutions to management.**
2. Use the steps in **"What good cash forecasting looks like."** Review whether the company
   adopts the stated discipline of **13 WCF or pro forma financial statements**. Identify
   issues or incomplete processes in the forecasting process and propose alternatives to
   management. **Submit a summary as homework.**
3. Review the **13-Week Cash Flow and Dynamic Financial Model templates**.

> Maji Safi's honest answer to part 2 is already written and is not flattering: the 13-week
> cash forecast does not exist. Bragg priority #1 in the Year-1 model says so in Renew's own
> words — *"Monthly is modelled; weekly is not… Single biggest gap today."* See D-05.

### Session 6 — Establish and evaluate internal controls · 28 Sep
Objectives: what internal controls are · basic concepts · control activities · checks and
examples · **self-evaluate your internal control policies**.

**Homework:** review the effectiveness of internal controls in your company using the control
checks, looking for loopholes · prepare a report of inconsistencies or observations and
**schedule a meeting with the CEO or senior manager to present it** · submit a summary.

### Session 7 — Costing · 5 Oct
Objectives: why costing matters · the various costing methods · the differences between them.

**Homework:** identify the applicable costing practices at your company · review whether they
comply with **IFRS rulings such as IAS** or related standards · identify costing issues ·
propose alternatives to management · submit a summary.

### Session 8 — Building and using a budget · 12 Oct
Objectives: what a budget is and why it matters · how a budget is developed and aligns with
other key corporate documents · why variance analysis matters and its structure · the steps
in variance analysis · **financial model walkthrough**.

**Homework:** review the budget practice in your company · review whether it adopts budgeting
best practices · identify issues · propose alternatives · submit a summary.

### Session 9 — Sustainable Finance / Review Session · 19 Oct · **bonus credit**
Objectives: evaluate the strategic impact of **climate risks and opportunities** on business
operations across African markets · integrate **climate-smart, gender-smart and waste
management practices (EMPs)** into core financial strategies · assess readiness for
**ESG-linked financing structures and standardised impact reporting (e.g. SEAF Scorecard)** ·
review concepts and ask questions to prepare for the final exam.

**Homework:** review the sustainable finance video modules · complete the **self-assessment
audit** · attempt the **practice exam**.

> Directly relevant to Maji Safi. Clean water in Kampala is the kind of business ESG-linked
> and blended capital is designed for, and this is the one session that carries bonus credit.

### Session 10 — CFO100 Exam · 26 Oct
Evaluation of knowledge and skills acquired from the programme. **50% of the grade.**

## The homework pattern

Every session from 5 onward follows the same four-beat structure:

> **review** the practice at your company → **identify** issues, gaps or misconceptions →
> **propose** alternative solutions **to management** → **submit** a summary

That is the syllabus teaching a habit, not testing knowledge. The CFO is the person who
audits the company's own practice against a standard and brings a recommendation to the
people who can act on it. Every remaining assignment is a rehearsal of that move on real
Maji Safi material.
