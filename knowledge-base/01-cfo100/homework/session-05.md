## Let's reflect

**What are your main takeaways this week with regard to cash management and forecasting?
Is there anything that is a significant change from the practices at your company
currently?**

> My biggest takeaway is that the 13-week and the 13-month forecasts answer two different
> questions. The 13-week asks "can we meet our obligations?" and the fix is timing: collect
> faster, pay on terms, defer spend. The 13-month asks "what can we afford to do next?"
> and the fix is a decision about the pace of expansion, not a timing trick. Until this
> session I had one annual model and treated it as both.
>
> The significant change is the weekly rhythm. We are pre-opening, so our practice has been
> to model cash monthly and look at it when something comes up. The session made clear that
> a forecast nobody rolls every week goes stale. You should never be surprised by your cash
> balance. We have now set a standing rule: every Monday morning, before anything else, the
> 13-week forecast is rolled forward and reconciled to the bank.
>
> The second change is about our own business model. Customers pay a refundable deposit on
> our 20L jar. That deposit is cash in our bank but it is not ours. A large part of our
> future closing cash will be customers' money, repayable when the jars come back. We
> now track "cash genuinely ours" separately from the bank balance and manage to that line.

**Which of the following cash management practices are currently in place at your
company?**

Tick **none**. Honestly, we are pre-opening and none are operating yet. If there is a
comment box, paste:

> None are operating yet, because we open in October 2026. Two exist as tools: a 13-week
> cash forecast and a 13-month forecast, both built in September in the Renew template
> structure. The others (bank reconciliation, CCC tracking, current ratio, a reserve) start
> when trading does. We plan a 3-day cash conversion cycle because every sale is cash on
> collection. We have no two-month reserve today, and that is our biggest cash gap.

**Are any cash forecasting processes incomplete? Have you observed any inconsistencies or
mistakes in how cash forecasting and management processes are applied? If so, what were
they?**

> Yes, several.
>
> 1. **Opening cash is assumed, not counted.** The forecast starts from a model figure of
>    UGX 4,000,000, not a reconciled bank balance. Everything downstream inherits that.
> 2. **We open below our own minimum.** Our policy floor is UGX 20,000,000. The forecast
>    opens about UGX 16,000,000 under it in week 1, and the plan's lowest month-end is about
>    UGX 17,500,000, still under the floor. There is no overdraft, facility or reserve
>    behind it.
> 3. **Timing is spread, not dated.** The first version divides three months evenly by 13
>    instead of placing each payment in the week it actually leaves the bank.
> 4. **Some cash movements are missing.** Jar deposit refunds are assumed to be zero, Local
>    Service Tax is not modelled, and there is no maintenance capex for membranes and
>    filters in a full year.
> 5. **Inconsistent numbers across our own files.** Our cost per site appears as three
>    different figures (USD 80,000, USD 20,000 and under USD 5,000), and the headline
>    capital ask was changed from USD 400,000 to USD 100,000 in outbound documents without
>    a management decision. If the inputs disagree, the forecast cannot be trusted.
> 6. **No one owns the books.** There is no bookkeeper or accountant yet, so there is
>    nothing to reconcile the forecast against.

**What changes do you recommend implementing at your company with regard to cash
management and forecasting?**

> 1. **Count real opening cash before we open.** Bank, mobile money and petty cash,
>    reconciled to statements, replace the model's assumption in week 1.
> 2. **Fix the week-1 breach before opening, not after.** We either inject about
>    UGX 16,000,000 of founder capital, arrange a facility, or slow the month-1 jar
>    programme. Whichever we choose, we decide it, not drift into it.
> 3. **Roll the 13-week forecast every Monday morning,** with each line dated to the week
>    cash actually moves. Reconcile closing cash to the bank every week.
> 4. **Update the 13-month forecast monthly,** and use it for the decision in front of us
>    now: our first hire.
> 5. **Report "cash genuinely ours"** (bank balance minus refundable jar deposits) as the
>    headline cash number, and never treat deposits as headroom.
> 6. **One source of truth for every figure.** Cost per site and the capital ask are
>    marked as disputed until finance confirms them, and no unapproved number goes
>    outbound.
> 7. **Appoint an accountant** so there are books to reconcile to from the first day of
>    trading.
