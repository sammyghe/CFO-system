## Let's reflect

**Which areas of your business require internal controls? What are the main risks that
are relevant to your company?**

> Maji Safi is a centralised water purification plant in Kampala selling 20L refills and
> single-use water to shops, institutions and households. We open in October 2026. The
> areas that need controls are:
>
> - **Cash and payments.** Today one person can initiate, approve, pay and record any
>   payment. Risk: unauthorised, duplicate or unrecorded payments.
> - **Customer jar deposits.** Customers pay a refundable deposit per jar. It is cash in our
>   bank that is not ours. Risk: deposits get spent as if they were profit, then cannot be
>   refunded when jars come back.
> - **Payroll.** We are making our first hires. Risk: pay agreed informally, by email, with
>   no reconciliation or independent approval.
> - **Tax.** VAT, excise and digital stamps are about 29% of gross billings and are collected
>   for URA, plus PAYE and NSSF. Risk: penalties, and spending tax money.
> - **Information we send outside the company.** Funders, lenders and partners receive our
>   figures. Risk: inconsistent or unapproved numbers going out. This has already happened.
> - **Systems and documents.** Risk: the wrong version of a key document gets used or sent,
>   and access is not managed.

**What types of internal control procedures do you have in place?**

> Few, and mostly designed rather than operating, because we have not started trading.
>
> - **A cash-mismatch block** in our internal operating system: a day cannot be closed if
>   cash does not match.
> - **A single source of truth for financial figures,** with an approval rule: no figure
>   leaves the company unless finance has marked it approved. Our automated agents must
>   check it before quoting a number.
> - **Daily automated briefs** that flag exceptions: stale files, conflicting figures and
>   overdue items. This works as a detective control.
> - **A written price book** with a B2B discount floor that must be earned on last month's
>   actual volume, never on a promise, and a rule that deposits are never discounted.
> - **Role-based company mailboxes** and an access register listing who should see which
>   financial view.

**Are there any areas where internal control procedures are missing? Are there any
existing control procedures that are not fully implemented? Why are these important —
what are the possible consequences?**

> **Missing:**
> - **Bank reconciliation, payment separation and monthly close: all zero.** One founder
>   controls payments end to end, and there are no books to reconcile. Consequence: we
>   cannot prove our cash balance is real, and an error or misuse would go unnoticed.
> - **Payroll approval.** Our first salary is being agreed by email with no documented
>   approval. Consequence: disputes, wrong pay, and no audit trail.
> - **Approval thresholds and vendor verification.** We are about to place our first jar
>   order with no approval limit and no check on supplier bank details. Consequence:
>   overpayment or a redirected payment.
> - **Document control.** Our Shareholders' Agreement exists in five identical versions on
>   Drive with no way to tell which is current, and has been unsigned for 116 days. A
>   capital tracker 39 days out of date and labelled "superseded" is the file a funder
>   would be sent. Consequence: governance disputes and losing a funder's trust.
>
> **Not fully implemented:**
> - **The figure-approval rule was bypassed.** Our headline capital ask was changed from
>   USD 400,000 to USD 100,000 and went into outbound documents without a management
>   decision. Our cost per site exists as three different figures. Consequence: two funders
>   can receive two different numbers, which damages credibility in a way that is hard to
>   recover.
> - **System access.** Company approvals happen on a personal Gmail address, our public
>   Google Business Profile was changed overnight with nobody verifying the changes, and
>   the website edit access is about to expire. Consequence: no record of who approved
>   what, and unverified claims on public surfaces.
> - **Key-person dependence.** Every control runs through the founder, who is currently
>   travelling. As the session put it, the system should not depend on one person's memory.

**What changes will you implement to address the inconsistencies you noticed? How can you
better support your teammates to improve the use of effective internal controls?**

> **Priority control: #1 Bank reconciliation** (30-day plan in the attached template). From
> our first week of trading, Ema prepares a weekly bank reconciliation and I review it every
> Monday with the 13-week cash roll. It is signed, filed in one Drive folder, and any
> difference is explained the same week. This makes our opening cash real and makes the
> forecast trustworthy.
>
> **Next, in order:**
> 1. **Payment approval with a small-team compensating control.** Ema initiates, I approve
>    each payment batch, and any payment over UGX 2,000,000 needs a second approver. I will
>    add a second bank signatory.
> 2. **Approval thresholds and vendor verification** before the jar order is paid: a
>    supplier's bank details are confirmed by phone on a number we already hold.
> 3. **Payroll:** approved salaries go into a written register signed by two founders before
>    the first payroll runs.
> 4. **Appoint an accountant** for the books and the monthly close.
> 5. **Enforce the figure-approval rule.** The capital ask and the cost per site are marked
>    disputed and pulled from outbound documents until the partners confirm them.
> 6. **Document control:** one signed Shareholders' Agreement, and older versions archived.
> 7. **Company accounts only** for company approvals. Website and Google profile access gets
>    listed with a named owner.
>
> **Supporting the team:** we will keep controls simple and written. Every control gets an
> owner, a piece of evidence, a reviewer and a frequency, on one page, so it does not live
> in my head. We are a small team, so we will use independent review instead of
> large-company bureaucracy: I approve batches, someone outside the transaction reviews the
> bank statement, and we review the controls together once a quarter. I will explain the
> reason behind each control, not just the rule. A control people understand is a control
> people keep doing.
