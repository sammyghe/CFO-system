# Maji Safi — Drawback Register

**Safiflow Ventures Group Limited, t/a Maji Safi** · Lukuli Road, Buziga, Kampala
Raised 21 September 2026 · Living document — update status, never delete an entry

## What this is

An honest list of what is wrong with Maji Safi as it stands today, written in the way Renew
Capital's CFO100 teaches a CFO to read a business: not "how are we doing" but "what breaks
this, and when."

**Source of every figure:** `majisafiyearone.xlsx` in Drive ("Maji Safi — Year One
Financials & Plans", prepared 8 September 2026), tab named in each entry. Nothing here is
estimated or inferred beyond that workbook and the Renew CFO100 emails. Where the workbook
itself flags something as open, this register says so rather than inventing a number.

**The standing caveat that governs all of it:** the workbook's own cover page says it —
*THIS IS A FORECAST, NOT A RESULT.* Maji Safi has not begun commercial trading. There is no
audited history and nothing to test the model against.

## How to read an entry

`Severity` · `Where it shows` (workbook tab) · `Why it matters` · `What would close it` ·
`Status` · `Raised`

The **What would close it** column is the backlog. Nothing in it has been acted on yet.

## Tier 1 — Will be asked in the room. No good answer today.

### D-01 · Nothing has traded
- **Where it shows:** Cover; Key Ratios
- **The finding:** Every figure in the workbook is projected. The Key Ratios tab reports
  ROE **51.9%**, ROA **44.0%**, ROIC **41.2%** for Year 1.
- **Why it matters:** A forecast ROE is an assumption with a percent sign on it. Presenting
  52% ROE to an investor as an achievement is the fastest available way to lose their trust
  — and the fastest way to keep it is to say the caveat before they do.
- **What would close it:** One month of actuals against budget. Until then, every ratio is
  quoted as *planned*, never as *is*.
- **Status:** OPEN — permanent until trading starts · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

### D-02 · No downside case exists anywhere
- **Where it shows:** whole workbook — by absence
- **The finding:** No sensitivity analysis, no stress test, no scenario switch. The plan
  contains exactly one future.
- **Why it matters:** "What happens at 60% of plan?" is the single most likely question in
  any finance room, and there is currently no page to turn to. A single-path model reads as
  optimism, not analysis.
- **What would close it:** A downside / base / upside scenario on the Year-1 model, with
  volume, price and jar-return rate as the switches.
- **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

### D-03 · The plan is below break-even in Month 1 by design
- **Where it shows:** Break-Even; P&L
- **The finding:** Month 1 plans **500 jars/day**. Full break-even including the jar
  programme is **577/day** (cash break-even 452/day). The Month-1 loss is **3,708,107 UGX**.
- **Why it matters:** The loss is arithmetic, not bad luck — and it assumes the 500/day
  ramp actually lands. At 350/day the month loses 3,900 units of contribution
  (3,900 x 1,616.82 = 6,305,598), turning the Month-1 result into a loss of roughly
  **10,013,705** — 2.7x the planned loss — while the jar programme cost stays fixed at
  8,250,000. *(Computed here from the Break-Even tab's Month-1 contribution per unit; it is
  not a figure the workbook states.)*
- **What would close it:** Either a Month-1 volume commitment that is contracted rather than
  assumed, or a deliberate decision to slow the jar programme in Q1 — the workbook notes
  slowing it "would make Month 1 profitable and Year 2 smaller." That trade is a founder's
  call and has not been made explicitly.
- **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

### D-04 · Lowest cash point has nothing behind it
- **Where it shows:** Cash Flow
- **The finding:** Lowest cash point **17,548,449 UGX**, Month 1 — "the thinnest the
  company ever gets." No overdraft, no facility, no committed investor tranche, no reserve.
- **Why it matters:** One delayed URA refund, one pump failure, or one month at 350 jars/day
  and the company is at zero. There is no second source of cash in the plan at all: the
  Cash Flow tab records no financing activity whatsoever.
- **What would close it:** An arranged facility (even unused), or a named minimum-cash floor
  with a written rule for what gets cut when it is breached.
- **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

### D-05 · The first six things a CFO does are all undone
- **Where it shows:** CFO 90-Day Plan (Bragg's 29 priorities)
- **The finding:** Priorities **1, 2, 3, 8, 9, 10** are all marked OPEN — forecast cash
  (weekly), daily bank reconciliation, vendor ledger, metrics, weekly sales report, weekly
  flash report. The workbook's own note on #1: *"Monthly is modelled; weekly is not. Build
  a 13-week rolling cash forecast and revise it every Friday. Single biggest gap today."*
- **Why it matters:** These are the operating spine of the role. Their absence is not a
  paperwork gap — it means nobody would see a cash problem until month-end.
- **What would close it:** Start with #1. A 13-week rolling cash forecast, revised every
  Friday. Then daily bank rec, then the vendor ledger before there are 200 invoices to
  backfill.
- **Status:** OPEN — 6 of 6 · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

## Tier 2 — Structural. In the numbers.

### D-06 · Customer deposits are the largest liability and are funded by growth
- **Where it shows:** Balance Sheet; Cash Flow; Pricing — Two Books
- **The finding:** Customer deposits reach **157,038,750 UGX** by Month 12 — the largest
  liability on the balance sheet. Closing cash of 444,665,391 includes that deposit money;
  cash genuinely the company's is **287,626,641**. There is **no jar return/attrition
  assumption, no refund reserve and no forfeiture policy** in the model. The workbook flags
  a third open item with the tax advisor: whether VAT crystallises on a forfeited deposit.
- **Why it matters:** Deposits in are recorded at 12.8m–13.4m every month and deposits out
  are never modelled. A return wave is pure cash out with no matching revenue. The workbook
  is right to warn: *"Never present it as equity, headroom, or distributable profit."* The
  risk is that it gets spent like it is.
- **What would close it:** A modelled jar-return rate, a ring-fenced refund reserve, a
  written forfeiture policy, and the tax advisor's answer on VAT.
- **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

### D-07 · The ramp is unvalidated and mechanical
- **Where it shows:** Cover; Break-Even; B2B Volume Ladder
- **The finding:** 750 new jar customers a month, flat, every month. Output 500/day in M1 to
  2,000/day in M12. No seasonality, no churn, no customer acquisition cost, no sales-capacity
  ceiling. The workbook's own priority #7: *"Lock the wholesaler list behind the 500/day
  target — the assumption the model rests on."*
- **Why it matters:** It is not locked. The entire year's profit sits on a ramp nobody has
  tested, expressed as a straight line.
- **What would close it:** Signed or verbally committed wholesaler volumes covering the
  500/day base, plus a churn assumption on the jar base.
- **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

### D-08 · DSO = 0 and DPO = 0 — the most fragile working-capital shape possible
- **Where it shows:** Key Ratios; Balance Sheet
- **The finding:** Trade receivables **zero** in every month. Trade payables **zero** in
  every month. DSO 0.0 days, DPO 0.0 days, cash conversion cycle 3.0 days (inventory only).
  Credit policy is priority #24 — OPEN.
- **Why it matters:** You pay everyone instantly and everyone pays you instantly. Every
  real-world deviation runs *against* cash, never for it. Institutional B2B in Kampala —
  schools, clinics, offices — will ask for 30 days. The moment one does, both the 3-day
  cash conversion cycle and the 17.5m Month-1 trough are wrong, in the same direction.
  There is no bad-debt provision either.
- **What would close it:** A written credit policy (the workbook's own draft rule: never
  above ~3%/month of sales, never to cash customers), a receivables line in the model, and
  a bad-debt provision.
- **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

### D-09 · Tax is known-incomplete and flagged rather than fixed
- **Where it shows:** Taxes; Cover ("WHAT IS STILL OPEN")
- **The finding:** Three live gaps, all flagged by the workbook itself:
  - **NSSF employer contribution recorded at 5%; statutory rate is 10%.** Against Year-1
    wages of 118,900,000 that understates employer cost materially.
  - **Local Service Tax (KCCA, on employees) — NOT IN MODEL at all.**
  - **Input VAT recovery assumed at 75%** with no EFRIS invoice discipline yet in place to
    earn it — while the entire first-hire business case rests on moving 75% → 90% (worth
    ~39,463,568/year before tax).
- **Why it matters:** Two of three understate cost; the third overstates a recovery that has
  to be *worked for*. All three run the same way: the plan is better than reality.
- **What would close it:** Confirm the NSSF rate before payroll goes live, add LST, and put
  EFRIS invoice collection in place before claiming the 75%.
- **Status:** OPEN — flagged in source, unresolved · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

### D-10 · Quarterly tax is lumpy and nothing ring-fences it
- **Where it shows:** Cash Flow; Taxes
- **The finding:** Corporate tax accrues monthly but is **paid quarterly**: 1,766,743 (M3),
  11,861,635 (M6), 28,991,408 (M9), **54,847,316 (M12)**. Year 1 total 97,467,102.
- **Why it matters:** The cash flow shows the spikes, but no tax reserve account exists.
  A 54.8m payment lands in the same month the business is celebrating its best trading.
- **What would close it:** A separate tax reserve account funded monthly with the accrual.
- **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

### D-11 · Capex and asset story is thin
- **Where it shows:** Balance Sheet; P&L; CFO 90-Day Plan #19, #28
- **The finding:** Property, plant and equipment **315,000,000** at cost, depreciated
  **63,000,000/yr** — a 5-year life. **No Year-1 capital expenditure of any kind** and no
  membrane, filter or consumables replacement modelled. No asset register mentioned.
  Risk #19 names *"consumables reserve spent on something else"* and leaves it unquantified.
- **Why it matters:** A UF plant running 2,000 jars/day consumes membranes and filters. A
  year with zero maintenance capex is a year the model is flattering itself, and the
  workbook has already named the failure mode without pricing it.
- **What would close it:** A maintenance capex line, a consumables replacement schedule, and
  a physical asset register with monthly inventory counts (#28).
- **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

### D-12 · Half the B2B price ladder is unapproved, and the floor rule is not a control
- **Where it shows:** B2B Volume Ladder; Pricing — Two Books
- **The finding:** Four bands. **List** (5,000 refill) and **Floor** (3,300 refill) are
  Sammy's decisions of 8 Sep 2026. The two middle bands — **500+** and **1,500+** — are
  marked *"PROPOSED — not approved"*, spaced evenly between the anchors.
- **Why it matters:** Part of the revenue mix is therefore assumption, not policy. And the
  discipline that protects the whole structure — *"the floor is earned on volume actually
  taken last month, never given for a promise"* — is a sentence in a workbook, not a control
  anything enforces at the point of sale.
- **What would close it:** Approve or replace the two middle bands; make the tier assignment
  a system check against last month's achieved volume, not a judgement call in the moment.
- **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

## Tier 3 — Organisational

### D-13 · Single site, single market, single point of failure
- **Where it shows:** Cover; Team & Hiring Plan
- **The finding:** Everything is Site 1, Lukuli Road, Makindye. Site 2 exists only as a
  hiring trigger.
- **Why it matters:** One plant, one city, one customer geography. Any interruption is total,
  not partial.
- **What would close it:** Not closable at this size — it is a risk to *state* in any
  investor conversation rather than one to hide.
- **Status:** ACCEPTED — disclose, don't fix · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

### D-14 · No auditor appointed
- **Where it shows:** CFO 90-Day Plan #15
- **The finding:** OPEN. *"No auditor exists. Appoint one and begin reviewed accounts —
  cheaper now than under a raise deadline."*
- **Why it matters:** The workbook already wrote the argument. It has not been acted on.
- **What would close it:** Appoint an auditor and begin reviewed accounts.
- **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

### D-15 · Finance is two founders wearing hats
- **Where it shows:** Team & Hiring Plan
- **The finding:** CFO today = Sammy, as a hat. First real hire (AP/AR clerk, 550,000/month)
  triggers at **650 jars/day** — above the Month-1 plan of 500/day.
- **Why it matters:** The hire that pays for itself (input VAT 75%→90%, ~39.5m/year) is
  gated behind a volume the business has not reached, so the recovery assumption in D-09 is
  unowned in exactly the period it matters most.
- **What would close it:** Either bring the AP/AR hire forward, or own EFRIS invoice chasing
  personally and put it in the weekly rhythm.
- **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

### D-16 · Certification and regulatory timing unquantified
- **Where it shows:** CFO 90-Day Plan #19
- **The finding:** *"Certification timing, single-buyer concentration, consumables reserve"*
  listed as "other risks" — none priced.
- **Why it matters:** A certification delay pushes the whole ramp right. A one-month loss
  becomes a one-quarter loss, and the 17.5m trough (D-04) is hit with less revenue behind it.
- **What would close it:** A dated certification critical path, and a modelled ramp-delay
  scenario (folds into D-02).
- **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

### D-17 · The model is a snapshot, not a live model
- **Where it shows:** Cover
- **The finding:** The workbook states it plainly: *"This is a snapshot… It does not
  recompute live — changing a number on this sheet will not ripple through the others."*
  Assumptions live in `build_statements.py` and the source model.
- **Why it matters:** "What if volume is 60%?" cannot be answered in the meeting — only
  after regenerating a repo. That is a CFO capability gap, not a tooling preference. The
  ability to answer a scenario question *live* is a large part of what the role is.
- **What would close it:** A scenario switch that recomputes, in whatever form is fastest to
  operate under pressure.
- **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

## Tier 4 — CFO readiness (the operator, not the company)

### D-18 · No external benchmark set
- **Where it shows:** Session 3 homework ("Ema sign", Drive)
- **The finding:** In his own words: *"I don't have reliable local industry benchmarks yet,
  so I'm comparing against our own budget and trajectory instead, not competitors."*
- **Why it matters:** Honest, and still unfixed. A CFO without an external benchmark set is
  grading his own exam. "Above the 15% healthy band" is a CFO100 rule of thumb, not a
  Ugandan packaged-water comparison.
- **What would close it:** A small benchmark set — Ugandan/EA packaged water and FMCG
  distribution: gross margin, net margin, asset turnover, typical credit terms.
- **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

### D-19 · Session 4 homework not done properly
- **Where it shows:** self-reported, 21 Sep 2026
- **The finding:** A known hole mid-course. The Session 4 topic and assignment are not in
  any email — they sit on the Renew training platform.
- **Why it matters:** CFO100 is cumulative; Session 5 (cash management) builds on what came
  before it.
- **What would close it:** Export the Session 4 material into the Drive folder
  `CFO 100 renew capital`, then redo it properly against Maji Safi's own numbers.
- **Status:** PARKED by decision, 2026-09-21 · **Raised:** 2026-09-21

### D-20 · The model is being read for good news, not for what breaks it
- **Where it shows:** Session 3 homework, compared against this register
- **The finding:** The Session 3 answers report 18% pre-tax margin as *"above the 15%
  healthy benchmark"*, ROE 52%, current ratio 2.9x, zero debt, 3-day cash conversion cycle,
  and conclude: *"On paper: strong."*
- **Why it matters:** Every one of those statements is true, and every one of them is a
  *forecast* whose fragility is catalogued above. D-01 through D-19 came out of the same
  workbook that produced the "strong" verdict. That inversion — reading the same page for
  what breaks it rather than for reassurance — is the mentality shift the whole CFO100
  track has to produce. It is the reason this register was written before anything else.
- **What would close it:** Practice. Every future model review starts from this register,
  not from the summary.
- **Status:** OPEN — the point of the exercise · **Raised:** 2026-09-21
