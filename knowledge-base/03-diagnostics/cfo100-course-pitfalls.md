# CFO100 — where the course breaks for Maji Safi

Asked for on 21 Sep 2026: *what pitfalls does that course have.* Written after reading the
syllabus, the Session 2, 3 and 4 decks, the Session 1 job descriptions, the Session 5
13-month cash model and Renew's Accounting and Finance Team Checklist.

**Stated plainly first: this is a strong course.** The LEAD framework, the D→A++ grading
ladder, the four-rooms discipline and the "ask why seven times" habit are worth more than
most paid finance training. The pitfalls below are not complaints about quality. They are
the specific places where a course built for **an operating SME with a finance team and a
CEO to report to** does not fit **a pre-revenue, founder-run, single-site water business in
Kampala** — and where following it literally would teach the wrong lesson.

---

## P-01 · The course assumes you have actuals. You don't.

Bucket 1 — Target vs Actuals — is the one Renew calls **"the accountable CFO."** Session 4
is built on variance analysis. Session 8 is budget-vs-actual. The GL Accountant job
description lists *"monthly expense variance analysis"* as routine work.

**Maji Safi has never traded.** There is no actual to put beside a target.

*The trap:* doing these exercises against the *forecast* and feeling they are done. A
variance against your own plan, computed from your own plan, is zero by construction.
Roughly a third of the course stays rehearsal until Month 1 closes. See drawback D-21.

*What to do instead:* treat Sessions 4, 7 and 8 as **pre-loading**. Build the report
template, the variance structure and the budget now, empty, so that the day Month 1 closes
the machinery already exists. That is a legitimate and honest use of the material.

## P-02 · It assumes you have a finance team. You are the finance team.

Session 1's assignment is five job descriptions — CFO, Accounting Manager, AP Clerk, AR
Clerk, GL Accountant. Session 2 teaches **segregation of duties** across custody, record
keeping, authorising and reconciliation. The Renew checklist assigns tasks to a Finance
Manager, a Senior Accountant and a Cashier. Session 3 says *"access to your accountant or
controller, so you can ask 'why?' seven times"* — then adds, almost as a joke, **"This might
be you."**

For Maji Safi that parenthesis is the whole situation. Finance is two founders.

*The trap:* **segregation of duties is arithmetically impossible with one person.** Reading
Session 6 (internal controls, 28 Sep) and concluding Maji Safi has controls because the
slides were understood would be the expensive version of this mistake.

*What to do instead:* compensating controls, not segregation — the bank statement as an
independent record, a second pair of eyes on anything above a threshold, and the Maji Safi
OS rule that blocks an end-of-day close on a cash mismatch. Name them as compensating and
say so out loud to any investor, rather than claiming a control structure that cannot exist
at this headcount.

## P-03 · It assumes you are not the CEO. You are.

This is the deepest one. The entire communication architecture — LEAD, four rooms, "send the
CEO a one-page summary by the 15th", the bad-news framework, "never let anyone be surprised
in public" — presumes **a CFO reporting to someone else.**

Sammy is founder and CEO. Write the monthly summary and you are writing to yourself.

*The trap:* the forcing function quietly disappears, and with it the discipline. This is
almost certainly why Session 4's homework came out thin (D-19). Nobody was waiting for it.

*What to do instead:* **manufacture the reader.** Renew is already in the room — Emily
Mahoney has an open thread. A monthly one-page LEAD summary sent to a real external party,
a prospective funder, a co-founder or an advisor, restores the pressure the course assumes.
Without a reader, LEAD is a writing exercise. With one, it is the job.

## P-04 · The benchmarks are generic, and not Ugandan.

Pre-tax margin *"10% floor, 15% healthy"*. Current ratio *"ideal 1.5x to 3.0x"*. Quick ratio
above 1.0x. For report templates the deck sends you to **SEC EDGAR** — a US filings
database. Renew's worked example, Kanzu Foods Ltd, reports in **USD** with a local-currency
side account.

Maji Safi is UGX-only, single-account, and its cost structure is dominated by items no
benchmark in the course contemplates.

*The trap:* Sammy already flagged this honestly in his Session 3 homework —
*"I don't have reliable local industry benchmarks yet."* The course does not supply them and
will not. Quoting "above the 15% healthy benchmark" to a Ugandan investor who knows the
packaged-water market invites the question *"compared to whom?"*

*What to do instead:* build the local benchmark set yourself. It is drawback D-18 and it
stays open.

## P-05 · Refundable customer deposits do not appear anywhere in the course.

Maji Safi's **largest liability** is the 20L jar deposit — 157,038,750 UGX by Month 12,
customers' money, repayable on return. The accounting treatment is subtle: a liability not
revenue, the jar cost expensed on issue, input VAT recovered, and an open question with the
tax advisor about VAT on a forfeited deposit.

Not one slide in Sessions 2, 3, 4 or the Session 5 model touches deposit liabilities.
Kanzu Foods' closing balance is entirely Kanzu's money.

*The trap:* Renew's cash model teaches you to manage to the **closing balance**. Do that at
Maji Safi and you will manage to a number that is 35% other people's money.

*What to do instead:* the workbook's 13-MONTH CASH tab carries two memo rows — *of which
customers' refundable deposits*, and *cash genuinely OURS*. **Manage to the OURS line.**

## P-06 · Uganda-specific tax is absent.

VAT at 18%, excise at the higher of 10% ad valorem or 50 UGX/litre, digital tax stamps at 15
UGX per unit, NSSF, PAYE, KCCA trading licence, Local Service Tax, EFRIS invoicing and the
input-VAT recovery rate that depends on it. Together, VAT and excise are **29.1% of gross
billings** — 740,623,047 UGX in Year 1 that never belongs to Maji Safi.

The course covers taxes generically ("Taxes / statutory payments" as one line in Kanzu's
model) and Session 7 covers costing against *"IFRS rulings like IAS"*.

*The trap:* a course-shaped P&L that reports gross billings as revenue. Maji Safi's model
already gets this right — revenue is stated net of VAT and excise per IFRS 15, with gross
billings as a memo line. **That is more rigorous than the course requires.** Don't let a
generic template talk you out of it.

## P-07 · The grading structure rewards attendance over the work.

**Exam 50% · attendance 40% · assignment completion 10%.** Certificate at 65%.

The homework is where the habits get built, and it is worth the least. You could attend
everything, pass a knowledge exam, collect a certificate, and change nothing about how the
business runs.

*The trap:* optimising for the certificate. Renew half-anticipates this — hence the mindset
slides, hence *"a report that doesn't get acted on did not happen."*

*What to do instead:* Sammy has already said he's handling submission. Fine. The measure
that matters is the operating rhythm, not the grade. Judge the course by whether the Monday
13-week forecast is still running in December.

## P-08 · It is a corporate-SME curriculum, not a startup-finance one.

Missing entirely: runway modelling from zero, cap table mechanics, dilution, instrument
choice, fundraising process, blended or catalytic capital, grant funding, unit economics
before scale, and how to present a business with no trading history.

Every one of those is live for Maji Safi right now. The Year-1 model's own note says actual
share capital, investor terms and valuation *"live outside this repository"* — the course
will not help fill that in either.

*What to do instead:* take the reporting craft from CFO100 and get the capital mechanics
elsewhere. Renew Venture Lab and the Angels Network are the adjacent products, and Sammy has
already asked Emily about the route in.

## P-09 · The session most relevant to Maji Safi is the one weighted least.

**Session 9 — Sustainable Finance** covers climate risk, climate-smart and gender-smart
practices, ESG-linked financing structures and the SEAF Scorecard. It is one session, near
the end, marked as a **review/bonus-credit** session.

For clean drinking water in Kampala, ESG-linked and blended capital is not a side topic —
it is plausibly the **main** funding route. Aqua for All, USADF and the Stone Family
Foundation are already named in the Year-1 model's own 90-day plan.

*The trap:* treating Session 9 as the optional catch-up session, which is exactly how it is
framed.

*What to do instead:* treat 19 October as one of the two most important dates in the course.

## P-10 · Variance analysis is taught without a materiality rule.

Session 4 demonstrates variance as Actual − Target in currency and percent, and prescribes
*"ask why seven times."* It gives **no threshold** for when a variance is worth
investigating, and no favourable/unfavourable sign convention for cost lines.

*The trap:* either chasing noise, or nothing — both look like discipline from the inside.

*What to do instead:* set your own rule before Month 1 closes. A sensible starting point for
Maji Safi: investigate any line more than 5% **and** more than 2,000,000 UGX off plan.

## P-11 · Presentation guidance without a chart framework.

*"Keep it simple: 5–10 charts maximum"*, *"use graphs where appropriate"*, *"a chart, not a
wall of digits."* No slide names which chart for which job — the only specific example is a
waterfall for an operating-expense bridge in Session 3.

Minor, but it means the A− → A++ jump on the grading ladder is under-specified.

## P-12 · Renew is an investor running a training programme. Know the frame.

Every email carries links to the Renew Venture Lab, the portfolio and the Angels Network.
CFO100 is free, funded by Global Affairs Canada and Norad, and it is also an ecosystem and
deal-sourcing channel.

This is **not** a criticism — it is the most useful fact about the course. It means
participation is visible, and the quality of your work in it is a signal to people who
deploy capital.

*The trap:* treating it as anonymous training.

*What to do instead:* behave as though the work is being read, because it is. That reframes
the thin Session 4 homework from an administrative matter into a missed signal — and the
13-week forecast Sammy walks in with tonight into a live one.

---

## The one-line version

CFO100 teaches you to be the CFO of a company that already exists. Maji Safi does not exist
yet. **Take the habits and the communication craft — they transfer completely. Do not take
the benchmarks, the team structure, the segregation of duties, or the assumption that
someone else is the CEO.**
