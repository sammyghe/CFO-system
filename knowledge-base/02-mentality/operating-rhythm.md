## THE STANDING RULE — Monday morning, 13-week cash

**Every Monday morning, before anything else: roll the 13-week cash forecast forward one
week and type what you actually expect to receive and pay. Then read the ALARM row.**

Sammy's rule, set 21 Sep 2026. It is supported on three sides:

- Renew's *Accounting and Finance Team Checklist* puts it on the **Finance Manager, weekly**:
  *"Prepare a weekly cash collection and disbursement plan and share with all finance staff
  (13 weeks cash flow)."*
- Session 4's meeting table lists a **Weekly 13-Week Cash Flow Meeting**:
  *"Review the end cash position with the forecast. Any difference? Any payments we need to
  slow or receipts we need to accelerate?"*
- The Year-1 model's own Bragg priority #1 calls its absence **"the single biggest gap
  today."**

The tool is `models/MajiSafi_OneSite_Model.xlsx`, tab **13-WEEK CASH**.

**Renew's distinction between the two horizons — learn this, it is the whole point:**

> The 13-week forecast answers **TIMING**: which payments do we slow, which receipts do we
> accelerate.
>
> The 13-month forecast answers **PACE**: can we afford the hire and the expansion at all.
>
> In Renew's own words on their 13-month model: *"unlike the 13-week model, the fix here is
> not a timing trick, **it's a decision about the pace of expansion**."*

## Before you hit send (Session 4)

> ■ Can I trust this data?
> ■ Does it cover what actually matters?
> ■ Is it still timely?
> ■ Is it clear enough to digest in under a minute?
>
> **"If the answer to any of those is no, it isn't ready."**

And the pre-send integrity checklist from Session 2 — *"BEFORE SENDING THE FS TO THE CEO"*:
cash check · net income check · receivables · payables · inventory · non-cash expense ·
interest · unusual variance → **OK TO SEND**.

## The anatomy of the monthly report (Session 4)

Four blocks, in this order:

1. **Executive Summary** — key issues + synopsis, up front, written as LEAD
2. **Action Plan** — what you'll do, not just what happened
3. **Financial Statements Analysis** — variances vs budget, explained
4. **Capital / Project Update** — progress, % complete, spend vs plan

**Production rules:** check the numbers carefully · **keep it simple, 5–10 charts maximum** ·
use graphs where appropriate · check spelling and formatting.

**Board deck protocol — 10 slides total:** 3 on variance analysis (sales, profit, cash — TvA,
month-on-month and year-on-year, with commentary) · 2 on ratios (performance, liquidity and
solvency, efficiency/CCC, with commentary) · 1–2 on financial risks, runway and mitigation ·
1 on changes in accounting policies · 1–2 on conclusions, recommendations or requests ·
1 on MD&A (what management plans next quarter or year, forecasts and capex).

## The report grading ladder (Session 4) — grade yourself before anyone else does

| Grade | What it does |
|---|---|
| **D+** | Restates the totals. *"Tells you nothing the spreadsheet didn't already say."* |
| **C+** | Adds a variance vs target. *"Still just narrating numbers, not explaining anything."* |
| **B+** | Adds percentages and names a driver |
| **A−** | Same content, rendered as charts and short bullets instead of a wall of numbers |
| **A** | A− **plus an explicit "Recommendation:" line** |
| **A++** | Renew's top grade |

> **"The jump from forgettable to great: name the driver, add the ask."**
