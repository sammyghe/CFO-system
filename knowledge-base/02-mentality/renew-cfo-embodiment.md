## 1. Character and habits — the part you asked about

Renew teaches this explicitly. It is not implied; there are slides on it.

### The two mantras (Session 4, pages 7–8)

> **"Great companies… Are run by great systems… Executed by good people, every day."**
>
> **"Great leaders… Are run by great habits… Executed every morning, rain or shine."**

### The morning routine (Session 4, page 9 — "Mindset Habit")

Renew's own list, verbatim:

1. **Acknowledge / "capture" your thoughts** — and practice a new belief instead.
   *"I'm not a disciplined person."* → *"I am learning to become more disciplined."*
2. **Get up 1 hour before others.**
3. **Drink a cold glass of water.**
4. **Read your personal vision statement out loud.**
5. **Journal — visualise your day:**
   - What am I excited for?
   - What would make today great?
   - What am I grateful for?
6. **Exercise for at least 30 min. Stretch for 5 min.**
7. **Have a place nearby to capture your ideas.**
8. **BONUS: write 3 "I Musts" for today on an index card.** Cross them out through the day.
   Write tomorrow's before you go to bed.

### Why they put this in a finance course (Session 3, page 5)

Of the **5 Habits of Executives** — *Mindset, Practice, Feedback, Focus, Time Management* —
Renew says **Mindset is the one everything else stands on**, and then draws the line
directly to the work:

> *"Your morning routine sets the mindset for the day. The same discipline that gets you up
> and focused is what gets your financial statements analysed every month, not once a year."*
>
> *"The CFOs who keep up with TvA, common-size and ratio analysis consistently are the ones
> who built the habit, not the ones who found extra time."*
>
> **"If you skipped a month of analysis, that is a mindset gap, not a math gap."**

That last line is the one to sit with. Renew's diagnosis of a missed month is not laziness
or workload. It is mindset. Everything in Session 3 *"assumes the discipline is already
there."*

### What the work actually requires (Session 3, page 12)

> 1. A spreadsheet, or printed statements and a calculator
> 2. **Two to four hours of uninterrupted time, every month**
> 3. Access to your accountant or controller, so you can ask "why?" seven times
>    — *"This might be you"*
> 4. A place to take notes
>
> **"This is not complicated, and it is not a skill you are missing. It is a habit."**

## 2. LEAD — the one framework everything else hangs on

Renew repeats LEAD in Sessions 2, 3 and 4. If you take one thing from CFO100, this is it.

| | | Say this, not that |
|---|---|---|
| **L**ead with the conclusion | What happened, and what do you think we should do? | NOT *"an update on receivables"* — BUT *"$400K is more than 90 days overdue and I recommend we do XYZ"* |
| **E**xplain the driver | Why? | NOT *"several factors"* — BUT *"three-quarters of it is freight"* |
| **A**mount at stake | How much? | NOT *"this will hurt margin"* — BUT *"$180K over six months"* |
| **D**ecision you need | What's next? | NOT *"we should follow up"* — BUT *"I need your decision by Friday"* |

**The test, from Session 2's summary:**

> **"If you cannot write the Amount line, you do not yet understand your own number."**

**Why you bury the answer** (Sessions 2 and 4 both):

| How you were trained | How an executive reads |
|---|---|
| Background → Scope → Method → Data → Analysis → **Conclusion** | **Conclusion** → Analysis → Data → Method → Scope → Background |
| *"A structure for proving you did the work"* | *"A structure for deciding"* |

Minto Pyramid Principle, McKinsey 1963. *"Working memory holds about four things, not seven."*

**"Nothing needed from you" is a legitimate D line.** Renew says so explicitly.

## 3. Four rooms, one number

> **"The number doesn't change. What they need from you does."**

LEAD never changes. Four things do: **how much detail, how far ahead, what you are asking
for, and how much uncertainty you show.**

Before you speak, ask two questions:
> **"What is this person deciding, and what are they afraid of?"**

What each reader is grading you on (Session 4, page 22):

| Reader | Grading you on |
|---|---|
| **Management** | *"Accuracy, and can I use this Monday morning"* |
| **Board** | *"Growth, and no surprises"* |
| **Lender** | *"Can this business pay what it owes"* |
| **Regulator** | *"Is the format right and the number defensible"* |

> **"None of them are reading for the same reason."**

The CFO relationship map runs in four directions — **Upward** (CEO, board, investors),
**Across** (HR, ops, IT, sales), **Outward** (lawyers, auditors, bankers, partners),
**Within** (your own finance team). Renew's warning:

> *"A CFO who only manages relationships in one of these four directions is not yet doing
> the whole job."*

## 4. Delivering bad news — the five steps

1. **Early beats complete**
2. **Lead with the fact, not the context**
3. **Bring the size of the hole**
4. **Two options and a recommendation**
5. **Never let anyone be surprised in public**

Renew's worked example of doing this *before* you have the number:

> *"We have an expense overrun... I don't have the final number yet, but it looks material,
> so I'm telling you now."* / *"Our current estimate is $75K–$100K over budget. I'll have the
> confirmed number by Friday."* / *"No decision needed today. I'll come back Friday with the
> final amount, the cause, and my recommendation."*

**Saying no without becoming the blocker** — never refuse, always price the trade:

> *"We can fund it if we push the vehicle purchase to Q1, or if we collect the top three
> overdue accounts. **Which do you want to trade?**"*
>
> **"Your job is to drive decisions and show people what the decisions cost."**

## 5. The five phrases that build trust (Session 2, page 33)

| Phrase | Why it works |
|---|---|
| *"I don't know yet. I will have that by Thursday at noon."* | Admit, commit to a time, deliver. **"Bluffing is fatal."** |
| *"I recommend."* | **"'The numbers suggest' is a report. 'I recommend' is an executive."** |
| *"Here is what I need from you."* | *"That is your D line, and it is the one people drop first."* |
| *"I'm not comfortable with that number yet."* | *"Shows judgment without pretending certainty."* |
| *"I missed that. Here's what I've changed so it doesn't happen again."* | *"Ownership + correction + control"* |

## 6. The three identities of the analysing CFO

Renew names a character for each analytical bucket:

| Bucket | Question | Who you become |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Target vs Actuals** | Are we hitting our numbers? Why or why not? Ask "why" seven times. | **The accountable CFO** |
| **2. Common-Size Analysis** | What are the trends? Any outliers? What are my margins doing over time? | **The wise CFO** |
| **3. Ratios and Margins** | How am I doing? How do we compare to ourselves last year, and to others? | **The performance, strong and healthy CFO** |

**Ask "why" seven times** — appears in three separate decks:
> *"The variance is the question. The seventh answer is the finding. The finding is what you
> say."*
> *"A gap on its own is not a finding. The reason behind it is."*

## 7. Checklists — Renew's theory of why you need them

> **"The smarter you are, the more you need a checklist — because you have more to remember."**
>
> *"Professionals who use checklists outperform those who rely on memory — that is why
> surgeons use them."*

**How to build one:** identify the pause points · write a check step at each · plain language ·
**no more than 15 steps** · **90 seconds to run** · **one named owner**.

## 8. Cadence is credibility

> **"Same day. Same format. Every month. Good news or bad."**
>
> **"A pack that arrives on the 10th every month gets read. A better pack that arrives
> whenever it is ready does not."**

- Close the books by a fixed date — **"Close on the 10th!"**
- Send the CEO a **one-page LEAD summary by the 15th**
- Same LEAD format every month
- Hold a short monthly financial review

**Don't wait for perfect:** if something is unknown, say so; give a date; **never delay the
whole report because one number isn't ready.**

**No surprises:** material bad news gets communicated when you know it, not at the monthly
report. **"The monthly meeting should never be the first time the CEO hears about a major
problem."**

Read against the drawback register, the embodiment gap is not analytical. The Year-1 model

| One-page LEAD summary to the CEO by the 15th | Does not exist. Sammy is the CEO, so no external reader forces it |
| Close on the 10th | No close process exists; not trading yet |
| *"A gap caught early is a decision. Caught late, it is an excuse."* | No actuals yet, so no gaps yet — the habit must be built before Month 1 closes, not after |
| Morning routine as the foundation | Unknown. This is the one item on the list nobody else can audit |

**The honest read:** Sammy is already a strong Bucket 2 and Bucket 3 CFO — the wise one and
the performance one. The Year-1 workbook does common-size and ratios to a standard the
course would grade well. What is missing is **Bucket 1, the accountable CFO** — and that one
cannot be built from a model. It needs actuals, a target, a monthly gap, and seven "why"s.

It also needs the thing Renew puts first and Sammy has not yet said out loud: the habit.
