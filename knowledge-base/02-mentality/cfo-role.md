## The supporting roles — what you are covering personally

Renew's Assignment 1 also defines the roles beneath the CFO. Every one of these is currently
a hat Sammy wears, which is the real meaning of drawback D-15.

**Accounting Manager** — manage and analyse funds · ensure accurate and timely monthly,
quarterly and year-end close · timely monthly reporting · assist with daily banking · support
budget and forecasting · improve procedures · bank compliance activities.

**AP Clerk** — review and verify invoices and check requests · sort, code and match invoices ·
set up for payment · enter into system · track expenses · process electronic payments · post
to journals and ledgers · reconcile AP · maintain **vendor files** · monthly reports and
month-end close.
> Maji Safi has **no vendor ledger** (Bragg #3 OPEN): *"Build one before there are 200
> invoices to backfill."*

**AR Clerk** — process accounts and incoming payments · day-to-day transactions (verify,
classify, compute, post, record) · prepare bills, invoices and bank deposits · reconcile the
AR ledger · resolve billing discrepancies · **send bill reminders and chase clients** ·
generate AR reports.
> Maji Safi models **zero receivables** (D-08). The day one institution asks for 30 days,
> this becomes a real job with no one in it.

**General Ledger Accountant** — month/quarter/year-end close · quarterly balance sheet and
P&L **fluctuation analysis** · monthly **expense variance analysis** · consolidation,
inter-company and foreign currency · balance sheet reconciliations · tax returns support ·
interface with other departments.
> Note that **variance analysis appears in the GL Accountant's job description too.** Renew
> treats it as routine, recurring work — not a special exercise. See D-21.

The Year-1 model's Team & Hiring Plan already prices these: AP/AR clerk at 650 jars/day
(550,000/month), Accounting/Finance Manager at 950 (1,200,000), GL Accountant at 1,400
(1,000,000), Financial Controller when Site 2 commits (2,500,000). Full Site-1 finance =
2,750,000/month ≈ 65 jars/day of break-even, 3.3% of capacity.

**Where you already are a CFO:** the planning and operations domains. You sit in the key
decisions, you built a traceable financial model, you put a control system in place that
blocks an end-of-day close on a cash mismatch. That last one is a genuinely CFO-grade
instinct — most founders at this stage do not build a system that refuses to lie to them.

**Where you are not yet:** **Funding.** Three NONEs and it is the domain that decides whether
the company survives its first thin quarter. Cash monitoring at a weekly cadence, an arranged
facility, and a policy for idle cash. Today's session is the first of those three.

**The one-line version:** you are already doing the CFO's *thinking*. What is missing is the
CFO's *rhythm* — the recurring, boring, dated obligations that turn a good model into a
managed company.
