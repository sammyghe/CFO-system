## FINANCE DESK ANSWER

**L —** The statements the brief is asking for do not exist, nobody keeps the books, and we
do not know our own year-end — so I cannot accept a December date yet. But the deeper point
is that these statements are **not** the capital asset. The **October close, due 10 November,
is.** I recommend we appoint an accountant this week and treat 1 October as the real deadline,
not December.

**E —** Safiflow was incorporated October 2024 and has never traded. FY2025 and FY2026 are
therefore two **pre-trading** periods. Prepared, they will show share capital, the plant and
pre-operating costs — and no revenue. They establish that the company is real, compliant and
that roughly UGX 315,000,000 of equity went into plant. That is a *credential*, not
*performance*. A lender sitting down in December 2026 will have **two months of trading** to
look at: October and November. That is what they will actually underwrite, alongside the
forward model. Those two months only exist if the source-of-truth file is live on 1 October
and the first close lands on 10 November.

**A —** Three things are at stake. The **USD 400,000** 2026 ask depends on this pack. Two
years of statutory accounts are apparently unprepared, which is an unquantified **URA
compliance exposure** — we cannot size it until the year-end is known, because the year-end
determines whether we are already late. And the plant already in the balance sheet,
**UGX 315,000,000**, is the main asset those statements exist to evidence.

**D —** Four decisions, this week, from Sammy:

1. **Establish the statutory year-end.** It is on the incorporation and URA registration
   documents. Everything else depends on it, including whether FY2026 can close before
   December at all. *(If the year-end is 31 December, FY2026 does not close until after the
   lender needs it, and the brief has promised something impossible.)*
2. **Appoint an accountant.** Not eventually — this week. This is Bragg priority #15 and
   drawback D-14, open since the 90-day plan was written.
3. **Confirm filing status with URA.** Are we late, and if so by how much?
4. **Agree the audit level.** See below.

## On "they don't need to be audited — audited is better"

For two near-dormant pre-trading years, a full audit is disproportionate, slow and expensive,
and there is almost nothing to audit — no revenue, few transactions, one large asset.

**Recommendation:** accountant-prepared statements for FY2025 and FY2026, with a stated
commitment to **audit FY2027**, the first real trading year. That reads well to a lender: it
says we know the difference between hygiene and evidence.

**Caveat:** a specific lender may simply require audited accounts. That is a question to ask
them, not a thing to assume in either direction. If one does, the cost and the timeline
change and this answer gets revised.
