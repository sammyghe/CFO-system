- Thinks big and global first ("act global from day one"), then wants it shaped into a clear plan. Likes gates, but pushes for speed.
- Learns by comparing to known global players: Oasis, Primo, Liquid Death, Grundfos, Xylem, Veolia, Davis & Shirtliff. Wants Maji Safi presented "the way the best companies do it".
- Wants clear, plain documents with one message per page, real photos, proof not claims.
- Wants to be asked a few sharp questions before anything is built. Then wants a clear proposal.
- Knows the China machine supply side well; trust his cost numbers on machines and test the rest.
- Writes fast with typos. Read for intent, not spelling.
- Dual narrative rule: commercial/debt audiences hear manufacturing and jobs; impact funders hear safe-water livelihoods. Never blend in one room.
- House voice: Tech Safari style. Short sentences, every number sourced and dated, verdict stated, honest seam named.
- Never print: landed machine cost (Uganda), prices in public, carbon-credit claims in partner packs, supplier names.

## 6. Ways to think for Maji Safi (lessons that carry across tasks)

1. **The water decides the technology.** Every project starts with a raw-water test and a demand number.
2. **Proof beats claims.** Lab reports, certification, a running plant. Name the weakness before the reader finds it.
3. **The machine is the small cost.** Room, tanks, distribution, brand and runway are most of the money.
4. **Capacity is rarely the limit; demand and source yield are.** Size the line to proven volume.
5. **Gate the spend.** Small now, bigger as each gate clears.
6. **Separate the bets.** New country ventures sit in their own entity with arm's-length licence/service fees back to Safiflow.
7. **Brand and distribution are the moat, not the filler.**
8. **Copy a deal shape that already exists** (method extractor rule).
