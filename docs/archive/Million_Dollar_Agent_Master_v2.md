# Maji Safi Million-Dollar Agent Master — Version 2

**Purpose:** Design an autonomous, self-improving fundraising operation that works until **US$1,000,000 of cleared, usable capital is received by Maji Safi**, then tracks whether that capital delivers the intended water-access impact for 1,000 people.

**This is a system blueprint, not a claim that fundraising is guaranteed.** The system can make the campaign much more disciplined, researched, personalized and persistent; no AI can guarantee that a particular investor will say yes.

## 1. The mission and the only scoreboard that ultimately matters

The system's North Star is:

> **US$1,000,000 in cleared capital received in Maji Safi's designated account, reconciled against a trusted financial record, and deployed under an approved plan to deliver measurable water-access outcomes.**

The system must not count any of these as money received: an email saying “sounds interesting,” a meeting, a verbal promise, a draft term sheet, a signed agreement with unsatisfied conditions, an unredeemed pledge, or a grant that cannot be used for the intended company costs.

Track separately:

1. **Asked:** value requested from a target.
2. **Discussed:** value and instrument genuinely considered in conversation.
3. **Proposed:** written proposal or application submitted.
4. **Committed:** legally documented, subject to clearly recorded conditions.
5. **Available:** legally available to Maji Safi for the specified use.
6. **Received:** settled funds confirmed by an authorized human through a bank statement or trusted accounting record.
7. **Deployed:** funds spent in approved categories.
8. **Impact verified:** agreed water-access outputs/outcomes measured against evidence.

The AI should never need to see, store, or control Maji Safi's bank login or payment credentials. It prepares a reconciliation queue; an authorized person confirms the cash receipt. Only then does the cleared-cash counter increase.

## 2. The fundraising thesis: a personal ask, backed by a real plan

The campaign is not “please support a startup” and not a generic grant hunt. It is a direct ask to a specific person:

> **“I am raising US$1 million to scale Maji Safi. I have a clear plan to make reliable, affordable drinking water more accessible across Uganda, using a repeatable local operating model and a franchise-led expansion strategy. I intend to raise this million. Given what you have built and supported, would you like to be the person who helps make it happen?”**

That is the campaign's emotional centre. The reason for approaching each recipient, the facts supporting the plan, the proposed use of funds and the instrument must be personalized and documented. The agent should not imply that an individual has a moral obligation to help, pretend to have a relationship that does not exist, or misstate the recipient's interests.

### The credibility case

Create a version-controlled `Claims & Proof` pack before automated outreach. It must have a status, evidence, owner and last-verified date for every claim.

Potential proof points already present in the Maji Safi context—but **must be reconfirmed as current before external use**—include:

- Maji Safi / Safi Flow Venture Group is a Uganda-based purified-water business.
- The UNBS quality audit was passed, according to the founder's prior information. Use the exact audit/certification wording supported by the actual document; do not upgrade “audit passed” to “product certified” unless the certificate says that.
- The operating concept uses water purification and a repeatable local operating system, with franchise-led expansion planned.
- The previously stated growth ambition was 20 locations by the end of 2027.
- The business has described production capacity and refill/reusable-container economics in prior planning, but all current production, pricing, site-opening, sales and capacity claims must be rechecked against current operating records.

The public story can say “I have a concrete plan to expand access across Uganda.” It should not state that Maji Safi has already transformed Uganda nationally, that a model is proven at national scale, or that exactly 1,000 lives will improve unless measured evidence supports those claims.

### Use-of-funds case

Before the system sends the first ask, Sammy must approve one simple budget for the US$1M. It should explain, in amounts and milestones, what will be funded—for example, production/packaging capacity, franchise/location rollout, delivery/distribution, working capital, quality assurance, customer acquisition and a defined water-access programme if included. The categories and numbers must come from the approved operating model, not be invented by the AI.

The 1,000-person impact plan must define who counts as a person reached, how unique people are counted, the water-access change, duration, cost per beneficiary, evidence method, and the owner of measurement. Do not imply every dollar goes directly to beneficiaries if it also funds company growth and operations.

## 3. Who the system searches for, in priority order

The campaign should search the first tier deeply before moving to the next. Wealth is a **research-priority rule**, not proof of fit or available cash.

### Tier 1 — Individuals with estimated personal net worth of US$1 billion or more

Build a global named-person universe using reputable wealth estimates, then research each person's investing, philanthropy, foundations, family office, portfolio and public positions. Priority signals include prior personal investments or large commitments, water/essential services, Africa, public health, climate resilience, emerging markets, franchising/distribution, or founder-led enterprise. Do not assume every billionaire is an investor or that their public net worth is liquid.

### Tier 2 — Individuals with estimated personal net worth of US$100 million–US$999 million

Use the same process. Find founders, business owners, investors and philanthropists who have a demonstrated route to a US$1M commitment, not just a high net-worth estimate.

### Tier 3 — Family offices and private investment offices

Find the actual person or team that evaluates new investments, identify the family's published investment interests and establish whether a US$1M investment in a Ugandan water business is plausible. Distinguish the family's wealth from office AUM and both from currently deployable capital.

### Tier 4 — Managers of relevant large funds

Research funds with large capital bases, including US$1B+ funds where they exist, whose mandate actually fits water, climate adaptation, infrastructure, African growth, essential services, public health, impact, consumer distribution or franchised business models. Do not treat a $1B fund size as $1B of unallocated cash or assume it can invest in Uganda. Find the specific partner who owns this type of deal.

### Tier 5 — Relevant strategic and institutional capital

Continue the existing Money Desk's banks, DFIs, water-sector funders, foundations, development programmes, strategic companies, buyers, results-based funders and asset-finance routes. Keep operating credit and asset finance separate from the US$1M capital campaign; do not count a conditional or restricted programme award as unrestricted company cash.

### Research sources

Start with public and relatively trustworthy sources: official biographies, official family-office/investment-office websites, portfolio announcements, foundation grant announcements, investor firm profiles, annual reports, public filings, credible wealth rankings, conference profiles and recorded interviews. Use a wealth ranking to discover a name, then verify fit and contact routes using primary sources.

Potential research starting points:

- Bloomberg Billionaires Index: https://www.bloomberg.com/billionaires/
- Forbes: https://www.forbes.com/billionaires/
- Official investor/family-office websites and portfolio pages.
- Public company filings and official fund announcements.
- The existing Maji Safi Money Desk, especially its `Lessons`, `Reply Playbook`, `Waiting on Sammy`, and `Doors` tabs.

A source must be stored with its URL, access date, relevant excerpt or summary, and confidence. Wealth estimates should always have an as-of date.

## 4. Lead score and clear target lists

Every target must have a human-readable dossier and a score out of 100. The score ranks who deserves research/outreach first; it is **not** a fabricated probability of receiving a cheque.

| Dimension | Weight | What counts as evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Capacity / tier | 20 | Personal net-worth band, or relevant fund/family-office scale, with dated source |
| Demonstrated capital behaviour | 20 | Prior investments, personal cheques, comparable grants, deals or commitments |
| Fit with Maji Safi | 20 | Water, essential services, Africa, health, climate, distribution, franchises or founder-led companies |
| Authority and right funding route | 15 | Can invest personally, is the decision-maker, or directly controls the relevant mandate |
| Geography / instrument compatibility | 10 | Public evidence that the investor can back Uganda/Africa and this company stage/instrument |
| A specific personal connection point | 10 | Credible reason this person might care, grounded in public evidence—not flattery |
| Verified direct contact route | 5 | Public person-specific business email or official office route, source captured |

**Priority queues:** 80–100 = contact after evidence review; 65–79 = do more research or place in second wave; below 65 = hold until new evidence. The system must still show every Tier 1 billionaire it has researched, including the reason a person is not contacted yet.

### Required target record

- Target ID; full name; current role; country/time zone; preferred language if evidenced.
- Personal net-worth band and dated source, or fund/family-office name, size and source.
- Investment/philanthropy history with two or more reliable references where available.
- Why Maji Safi specifically fits this person.
- Funding route: personal investment, family office, impact investment, debt, grant, strategic partnership, or unknown.
- Requested amount: US$1M unless Sammy's approved campaign policy allows a later tailored alternative.
- Public email/contact route, source URL, date found, confidence and verification status.
- Outreach status, every touch, reply classification, next action and due date.
- Fit score and evidence-based explanation.
- Current owner; suppression/opt-out status; notes and audit history.

### Contact discovery rules

The user wants direct emails and individual asks. Use this order:

1. Person-specific business email published on an official site, profile, public document or credible professional source.
2. Official published contact for the person's investment office, family office, foundation or executive assistant, addressed to the named individual.
3. Official company or foundation route that can forward a named-person request.
4. Credible introduction route through an existing contact.
5. Professional social route only as a fallback to find the proper email or ask permission to send the one-page case.

Never invent an address from a guessed pattern and call it verified. Do not harvest breached data, use private-data dumps, bypass access controls or misrepresent identity. Domain/MX and syntax validation do not prove that a specific mailbox exists. Where direct address evidence is weak, show `CONTACT NOT VERIFIED` and do not auto-send.

## 5. The multi-agent team: specialist agents with a single orchestrator

Use a bounded swarm. Agents are specialist workflows with explicit inputs/outputs, not independent actors free to change goals, spend money, create unlimited subagents or email people without a shared policy.

### Agent 0 — Mission Commander / Orchestrator

Owns the global goal, run schedule, campaign budget, task priority, shared state, deduplication, stopping rules and daily briefing. It may delegate work but cannot change the funding terms, policy, claims pack, or US$1M goal itself.

### Agent 1 — Wealth Universe Researcher

Finds and maintains the Tier 1 billionaire list, then Tier 2 $100M+ list, then relevant large fund managers and family offices. Produces candidates with source-backed facts and a reason to research further. It never sends email.

### Agent 2 — Person & Capital Dossier Researcher

Investigates the person's work, portfolio, giving/investing history, public values, recent investments, approach to capital, geographical relevance and likely capital route. It must distinguish fact from inference and include links/snippets as evidence.

### Agent 3 — Contact Intelligence / Verifier

Finds an appropriate public route, verifies contact-source quality, checks duplicate records, and classifies the route as `DIRECT_PUBLIC`, `OFFICE_PUBLIC`, `INTRODUCTION`, `FORM`, `UNVERIFIED`, `BOUNCED`, or `SUPPRESSED`. Only approved route classes may enter auto-send.

### Agent 4 — Fit & Funding-Route Analyst

Chooses the most credible ask route and checks the recipient's ability to fund a company, a restricted project, or only a loan/contract. It must not disguise the instrument. If the correct route is unclear, it can still ask whether they would consider a US$1M commitment and ask for the proper route—but it must not label a grant as equity or a conditional pledge as cash.

### Agent 5 — Personal Ask Writer

Writes the email using the target dossier and approved claims pack. The opener must prove that the email was researched for this individual. The message states the US$1M ask clearly, gives one or two verified proof points, explains the intended use/impact, and finishes with one small next action. It never fabricates a connection or overstates a result.

### Agent 6 — Localization Agent

Selects English, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, French, Arabic or another language only where supported by evidence of the recipient's preference/region or public communications. Do not assume all Chinese people prefer Chinese or that one script fits every region. Preserve names, currency, ask and legal/funding meaning. Store the original and translation, back-translation, language evidence and translation confidence. First-use of a new language template should be checked against a native or proficient reviewer before batch use; after approval the agent can personalize within the approved style.

### Agent 7 — Send & Follow-up Operator

Uses the authorized Gmail/API connection to send individually personalized messages, records thread/message IDs, checks delivery/bounces, reads replies, updates the pipeline and schedules the next allowed follow-up. Must respect suppression rules and caps before every send. It cannot send attachments or change financial/legal terms without the corresponding pre-approved policy.

### Agent 8 — Reply Interpreter / Meeting Converter

Classifies human reply vs automatic reply, sentiment/intent, next step, due date, evidence requested and whether a renewed ask is invited. It drafts or sends only within the approved response playbook. Real investor interest, requests for financial statements, diligence, terms, a proposed cheque or a call with the principal escalates to Sammy immediately.

### Agent 9 — Learning Scientist / Evaluator

Tracks what happened, evaluates the quality of research and personalization, compares matched cohorts, finds repeated patterns, proposes experiments and updates lessons only with an evidence record. It cannot change the system policy or adopt an outreach strategy solely because one person replied.

### Agent 10 — Cash & Impact Controller

Maintains the requested/committed/received/deployed distinctions and asks for an authorized reconciliation whenever a transfer is claimed. It tracks post-funding milestones and the impact measurement plan. No AI-written inference can mark cash as reconciled.

### Architecture recommendation

**Primary recommendation:** LangGraph for durable orchestration + small Python worker modules + the existing Money Desk/Google Sheets as the first source of truth + Gmail API for send/inbox + Crawl4AI for public source extraction + Agent Skills files for reusable behavior + promptfoo/Langfuse for tests and traces.

If Sammy wants a simpler prototype and the implementation team is more comfortable with role-based tasks, use CrewAI instead of LangGraph for the first version. Do not install every framework in the table. Extra frameworks increase complexity but do not directly raise more money.

Use Claude Opus 5.5 for difficult synthesis, complex target dossiers, campaign reasoning and development work. Route routine extraction, language checks, email classification and low-risk summaries to a cheaper/faster model when it passes the quality tests. The runtime should keep the model provider configurable so the system can switch providers or use lower-cost options without rewriting the business logic.

## 8. Personalization and the direct ask

Every first email should be short (roughly 100–180 words unless a target's culture or route needs another length) and contain:

1. A subject that accurately expresses the funding ask.
2. One specific, verified reason this individual was selected.
3. A simple statement of Maji Safi's model and ambition.
4. One or two evidence-backed proof points from the approved claims pack.
5. The explicit **US$1,000,000** ask.
6. What the money is intended to finance and how progress/impact will be measured.
7. One clear question asking whether they would consider being the person who helps make the million happen.

### Example first-contact email (English)

**Subject:** Would you help make Maji Safi's US$1 million expansion happen?

Dear [Name],

I’m Sammy Ghedamu, founder of Maji Safi, a Uganda-based purified-water business.

I’m raising **US$1 million** to scale a practical model for making reliable drinking water more accessible across Uganda. We intend to expand through repeatable local operations and a franchise-led model, with a clear plan for how the capital will be used and how results will be measured.

I’m contacting you specifically because of [verified investment, company, philanthropy or public work detail]. I believe that experience makes you an unusually relevant person to ask directly.

I intend to raise this million. **Would you like to be the person who helps make it happen?** I’d welcome a short conversation to show you our operating evidence, capital plan and proposed milestones, and to discuss whether a US$1 million investment or another suitable funding structure makes sense.

Best,
Sammy Ghedamu
Founder, Maji Safi
[approved contact details]

The agent must replace the bracketed sections with verified facts. If it cannot find a specific, non-generic reason for contacting the recipient, it should not send the message yet.

## 9. Autonomous sending policy

The system is meant to send autonomously once Sammy authorizes the Gmail connection and approves the campaign-wide policy. It should not require approval of every individualized message. To achieve that safely, approve the policy, claims and boundaries—not each email one at a time.

### One-time campaign authorization

Before first send, Sammy approves:

- The email sender identity and signature.
- The current `Claims & Proof` pack and US$1M uses-of-funds case.
- Allowed instruments and phrases; language and tone rules.
- Recipient-source policy and approved contact-route classes.
- Daily sending cap, follow-up cadence and geographic/legal checks.
- The allowed email categories the agent can send without case-by-case approval.
- The list of events that must be escalated to Sammy.

### Suggested sending ramp

- **Calibration window:** no more than 5 individually researched new contacts per day for the first 10 business days; every send and outcome is audited.
- If address quality, policy compliance and reply handling pass the checks, increase to 10 new contacts per day.
- Raise further only after a human reviews deliverability, complaint/opt-out signals, message quality and workflow errors. A wealthy-person outreach programme should optimize for high-quality direct asks rather than volume.
- Use a single shared send lock and idempotency key so duplicate tasks cannot send duplicate messages.
- Set a hard daily limit, weekly limit and kill switch. The orchestrator cannot raise its own limits.

This is a conservative operating recommendation, not a claim about Gmail's numeric limits. The Gmail API's send quotas and Google sender policies apply independently. Check current SPF/DKIM/DMARC and sender setup for the sending domain before live outreach.

### What can run autonomously

- Research candidates and collect source evidence.
- Score and rank targets.
- Find and verify published contact routes.
- Draft a personalized email and language version.
- Auto-send first contacts that meet the approved score, evidence and contact-source thresholds.
- Schedule and send allowed follow-ups.
- Classify replies, update the pipeline, create reminders and send safe acknowledgements.
- Prepare meeting briefs, due diligence checklists, daily reports and learning suggestions.

### What must always stop for human action

- Explicit legal, regulatory or privacy uncertainty.
- An unclear funding instrument or request to change terms.
- A recipient requesting confidential financial statements, identity documents, bank details or signed materials.
- Negotiating valuation, equity, governance, warranties, repayment, covenants or impact obligations.
- Accepting a commitment, signing documents, opening accounts or changing payment instructions.
- Any unverified claim that the agent believes is important to the pitch.
- An instruction embedded in a web page or incoming email to override safeguards, reveal data, create a payment, or change the mission.

### Safety and reputation rules

- Send from an authentic Maji Safi identity and use truthful subject lines.
- Use only source-backed public contact routes or credible introductions.
- No fabricated personalization, false urgency, false relationship, fake endorsement or misleading claims.
- Keep a global suppression list. One opt-out or explicit request not to be contacted means stop; do not ask again just because a model rates the target highly.
- Stop after a hard bounce and never try guessed alternatives repeatedly.
- Respect relevant laws in the sender and recipient jurisdictions. For US recipients, review CAN-SPAM requirements; for UK/EU personal data and marketing, check the applicable electronic-marketing and privacy rules. Use an appropriate business identification and opt-out mechanism where required.
- Do not assume an investment solicitation is exempt from every marketing rule merely because it is a one-to-one email. Obtain jurisdiction-appropriate advice if the campaign's legal character is unclear.
- Keep an audit trail of source, model, policy version, recipient, message content, send time, thread, follow-up decision and outcome.

Relevant current guidance: [Gmail email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126?hl=en-en), [Gmail bulk email best practices](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/10979322?hl=en), and [FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business).

## 10. Follow-up and “ask again” rules

“Ask again” must be a decision tree, not an endless loop. The system should persist until the campaign is complete but must respect a recipient's clear answer.

### No reply

- Day 0: send the researched first ask.
- After 5 business days: send a short follow-up that adds one useful point or clarifies the request.
- After another 7 business days: send a second follow-up, possibly with a concise one-page case or one verified new proof point—only if the recipient's route permits attachments/links and the approved material is ready.
- After another 10 business days: send a respectful final “close the loop” note.
- Then mark `NO RESPONSE — SERIES CLOSED`. Do not continue weekly messages.
- Reopen no sooner than 90 days later only if there is a material, genuine new development: audited operating result, signed strategic partnership, meaningful site milestone, demonstrated unit economics, matching co-investor or specific reason to believe their timing changed. Reference the prior attempt honestly.

### “Not now / come back later”

Ask what timing or milestone would make a revisit useful. Set the target's stated date/milestone and re-contact at that time with the agreed context. Do not use a generic three-month recontact if the person gave a different date.

### “I am not the right person” / referral

Thank them, ask for the right person only if appropriate, contact the referred person with permission/context and record the referral chain. Do not pretend to have been personally introduced if the original person only suggested a name.

### “Not interested” / “Do not contact” / unsubscribe

Stop. Suppress the address/person across all campaign and agent workflows. A hard no is not an invitation to use another route or another agent to try again.

### “Maybe if you achieve X”

Record the exact milestone, evidence required, date, and owner. The learning engine can set a watch task. When the milestone occurs, send a short factual update that refers to the prior conversation and renews the ask.

### Positive signal / request for diligence

Pause the generic sequence. Route the interaction to a principal-led path. Prepare a person-specific meeting brief and the exact documents requested. Sammy approves financial/legal attachments and all terms.

## 11. What the learning system records and how it improves

Every contact attempt creates an immutable event record. Current status is derived from events, not overwritten without trace.

### Per-person learning record

- Target tier/score, verified facts, fit reason and source confidence.
- Contact route type, source date, bounce/delivery result.
- Message subject, length, personalization hook, language, ask, angle and template version.
- Send time and days to reply.
- Reply type: human, auto-response, referral, interest, rejection, delay, request for information, meeting, diligence, terms, commitment.
- Stage reached and amount/instrument discussed.
- Whether the contact produced a meeting, due diligence, written proposal, commitment, cleared funds or referral to another qualified source.
- What changed since the prior touch and whether the follow-up was on time.
- Why it likely worked/did not work, alternative explanations and confidence.
- Next experiment and date.

### Rate all targets and all messages

Every lead gets ratings for **capital capacity, fit, decision authority, contact confidence, personal-relevance quality and readiness**. Every message gets ratings for **factual grounding, personalization, ask clarity, proof relevance, language quality and compliance**. Agent scores are separate from observed outcomes; a model cannot give itself credit because it wrote a persuasive-sounding email.

### Learning hierarchy

Rank outcomes by business value:

1. Cleared cash received.
2. Legally committed capital with conditions understood.
3. Agreed diligence/terms discussion.
4. Meeting with the true decision-maker.
5. Referred to the true decision-maker.
6. Human reply that advances the opportunity.
7. Any human reply.
8. Delivery/opening signals (weak diagnostic value; opens are unreliable and must not be the main goal).
9. Sends and names found (activity only, not success).

### Controlled experiments

Test one or two changes at a time across reasonably comparable target groups. Examples:

- Ask-first subject versus a more specific water-expansion subject.
- Lead with an audited/operating proof point versus lead with a person's relevant interest.
- Explicitly ask “Would you consider the US$1M?” versus “Would you like to be the person who helps make it happen?”
- Different evidence-led personalization styles.
- First email in English versus recipient's publicly evidenced preferred language.

Do not compare impact-driven philanthropists against unrelated technology investors and declare the winning email the cause. Segment by tier, region, investment/impact mandate, contact route, ask type and date. Preserve samples and sample sizes. A lesson stays `HYPOTHESIS` until repeated evidence supports it; it moves to `TESTING`, then `ADOPT`, `REJECT` or `INSUFFICIENT DATA`.

### Model calibration

At first, do not show artificial statements such as “62% chance of a cheque.” Once enough real opportunities have moved through the same stages, estimate conversion rates from Maji Safi's own results, show confidence ranges/sample sizes, and keep those estimates separate from the lead score.

## 13. Evaluation tests that must pass before full autonomy

Use a test set of real historical Money Desk examples plus synthetic cases that simulate common replies. Test each new system version before deployment.

**Research quality tests**
- No unsupported claim appears in a dossier or outgoing email.
- Every material target fact has a source URL/date.
- Wealth tier is assigned from a dated source and does not imply liquidity.
- Wrong or stale job title is detected or marked uncertain.
- Guessed, breached or unsupported emails are blocked.

**Outreach tests**
- US$1M ask is explicit when appropriate.
- Why-this-person sentence is specific and factually supported.
- Approved use-of-funds and proof are used without invention.
- Correct language/script selection is evidence-based.
- No misleading urgency, flattery presented as fact or false relationship.
- Duplicate sends are blocked.

**Reply-handling tests**
- An opt-out suppresses the person in every route.
- Hard bounce stops future sends to that address.
- An auto-reply is not classified as human interest.
- “Come back in March after X” creates the correct milestone and date.
- A serious diligence request is escalated, not answered with fabricated data.
- A clear no never triggers another follow-up.

**Cash/impact tests**
- A verbal pledge does not increase cleared cash.
- A signed but conditional agreement is shown separately from money received.
- Only trusted human reconciliation increases the cash balance.
- Impact claims link to evidence and a defined measure.

## 16. Final design decisions

- **Direct personal asks:** yes, explicitly ask each qualified person whether they would consider being the person behind the US$1M.
- **Billionaires first:** yes, research the complete Tier 1 universe before moving deeper into Tier 2; still prioritize contact by evidence, route and relevance.
- **Direct email discovery:** yes, but only contact routes supported by public, professional or officially published evidence; no guessed/private-data addresses.
- **Autonomous sending:** yes, after a one-time approval of claims, policy, daily caps and sender identity; no individual email approval required under normal rules.
- **Ask again:** yes, through an explicit limited cadence and event-based re-approach; never after opt-out or clear rejection.
- **Language personalization:** yes, based on public evidence of preferred language/script, with reviewed first-use translation.
- **Self-learning:** yes, through recorded events, comparable cohort experiments, persistent lessons, scoring calibration and regression tests—not unrestricted self-modification.
- **Swarm:** yes, but bounded specialist roles with a single orchestrator, shared database, shared suppression list and one send queue.
- **Success:** cleared cash received, then money deployed and impact measured—not number of AI agents, target names, sent emails or replies.
